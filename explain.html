How the opponent reads you

Rock paper scissors is solved if both players throw each move with equal probability. The hard part is that people usually leave small traces: repeating after a win, correcting after a loss, or cycling away from a move they have used too often.

The model is an ensemble, not a mind reader

Every round starts by asking several small predictors what your next throw might be. One predictor counts your whole history. Another values recent throws more. Two Markov predictors ask what usually follows your last one or two throws. A response predictor watches what you do after wins, losses, and ties.

The strongest move in that distribution is treated as your likely throw. The AI then plays the move that beats it. Its weight update is intentionally simple: a predictor that called recent rounds correctly earns more influence; a predictor that missed fades.

Why it is hard

Many human players are close to random in the first few rounds, then slip into conditional responses. In a large experimental study, players showed cycling and conditional patterns related to outcomes, including win-stay and lose-shift behavior [1]. The app exploits that kind of leakage, but only after it appears in your own match history.

If your sequence stays near the mixed-strategy equilibrium, the model has little to use. Randomizing equally across rock, paper, and scissors makes every pure counter equally risky [2].

Why it is fair

The click handler asks the AI for its throw before the current human throw is scored. The AI cannot peek at the button you just pressed. It can only punish patterns already visible in completed rounds.

  1. Read completed rounds.
  2. Predict the next human throw.
  3. Lock the AI throw.
  4. Reveal and score both throws.
  5. Update predictor accuracy for the next round.

Glossary

P(m)
The final probability assigned to move m, where m is rock, paper, or scissors.
w_i
The influence of predictor i, raised when that predictor has been accurate.
p_i(m)
The probability that predictor i assigns to move m.
sum w_i
The total influence across active predictors, used to keep probabilities adding to one.
